# Placement, Layering and Integration

**Domain:** AML · **Typology:** money-laundering-stages · **Status:** internal reference (synthetic)

## The three stages

Money laundering is conventionally described in three stages. Investigators must place any
observed activity in the correct stage, because the applicable typologies and monitoring
rules differ by stage.

1. **Placement** — introducing illicit cash into the financial system. Techniques include
   **structuring** and **smurfing** (both threshold-avoidance) and cash-intensive business
   commingling. This is where physical cash first becomes a balance.
2. **Layering** — separating proceeds from their source through chains of transfers,
   conversions and cross-border movement. Complexity, not amount, is the signal.
3. **Integration** — returning laundered value to the economy as apparently legitimate
   wealth: property purchases, business investment, loans back to the launderer.

## Why the distinction matters for SAR narratives

A SAR narrative that names the wrong stage is weak evidence. Structuring belongs to
placement; a pass-through chain belongs to layering; a sudden real-estate purchase funded
by opaque inflows belongs to integration. State the stage, then the typology, then the
specific rule the activity breached.
