# Layering

**Domain:** AML · **Typology:** layering · **Status:** internal typology definition (synthetic)

## Definition

Layering is the second stage of money laundering, after placement. It is the process of
**separating illicit proceeds from their source** through complex chains of transfers,
conversions and movements — between accounts, institutions, instruments and jurisdictions
— so that the audit trail becomes hard to follow. The defining feature is **obfuscation
of origin through movement**, not threshold avoidance.

## Distinguishing signals

- Rapid in-and-out transfers ("pass-through" or "U-turn" activity) with little or no
  balance retained.
- Funds converted between products (wire → e-money → crypto → wire) without economic
  purpose.
- Chains crossing multiple jurisdictions, especially through higher-risk corridors.
- Shell entities or nominee accounts inserted purely to add hops.

## How it differs from neighbouring typologies

Layering is frequently mislabelled as **structuring** or **smurfing**, but those are
placement techniques defined by keeping amounts under a reporting threshold. Layering can
involve large, fully-reported transactions — its purpose is **distance from origin**, not
staying below a line. It is also distinct from **trade-based laundering**, which uses
mis-invoiced trade to move value; layering moves the money itself.

## Internal threshold reference

The layering rule fires on three or more pass-through hops where inbound and outbound
amounts match within 2% and the balance is held for less than 48 hours. Escalate to SAR
readiness review; human approval required.
