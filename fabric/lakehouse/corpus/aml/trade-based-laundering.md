# Trade-Based Money Laundering

**Domain:** AML · **Typology:** trade-based-laundering · **Status:** internal typology definition (synthetic)

## Definition

Trade-based money laundering (TBML) moves value by misrepresenting the price, quantity or
quality of goods and services in trade transactions. The defining feature is that the
**money movement is disguised as legitimate trade** — the illicit value rides inside
invoices, letters of credit and shipping documents rather than through a bare transfer.

## Distinguishing signals

- Over- or under-invoicing: invoice value materially diverges from fair market value.
- Multiple invoicing of the same shipment, or phantom shipments with no goods at all.
- Goods description inconsistent with the trade corridor or the customer's stated business.
- Payments routed through third parties unrelated to the underlying trade.

## How it differs from neighbouring typologies

TBML is distinct from **layering**: layering moves the money itself through hops, while
TBML embeds value inside trade paperwork. It is also not **structuring** or **smurfing**,
which are threshold-avoidance placement techniques. The discriminating question is whether
a *trade instrument* is the vehicle.

## Internal threshold reference

The TBML rule fires when invoiced unit price deviates more than 30% from the reference
price band for the commodity and corridor, or when settlement counterparty differs from
the named trade counterparty. Escalate to SAR readiness review; human approval required.
