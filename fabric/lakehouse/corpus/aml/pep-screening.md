# PEP and Adverse Media Screening

**Domain:** AML · **Typology:** pep-screening · **Status:** internal reference (synthetic)

## Definition

A politically exposed person (PEP) holds, or is closely associated with someone who holds,
a prominent public function. PEP status does not imply wrongdoing, but it raises the money-
laundering and corruption risk and mandates enhanced due diligence. Adverse media screening
complements this by surfacing negative news that may indicate risk.

## Screening signals

- Match against PEP lists (domestic, foreign, international organisation) and their close
  associates and family.
- Adverse media hits linking the subject to fraud, corruption, sanctions evasion or
  organised crime.
- Source-of-wealth and source-of-funds inconsistent with the known public role.

## Use in AML narratives

PEP status is **customer context**, not a typology. It sharpens the assessment of an
otherwise ambiguous pattern: unexplained wealth moving through a PEP-linked account raises
suspicion of **integration** or **layering**. Name the typology separately; use PEP status
to justify the enhanced scrutiny and the readiness decision.

## Controls

Enhanced due diligence, senior sign-off on the relationship, source-of-wealth verification,
and ongoing monitoring. Escalate anomalies to SAR readiness; human approval required.
