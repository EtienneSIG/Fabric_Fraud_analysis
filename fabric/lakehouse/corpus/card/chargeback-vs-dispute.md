# Chargeback versus Dispute

**Domain:** Card · **Typology:** chargeback-dispute · **Role:** distractor (synthetic)

## Definition

A **dispute** is a cardholder's initial claim that a transaction is wrong. A **chargeback**
is the formal reversal of funds through the card scheme that may follow an unresolved
dispute. The two terms are routinely used interchangeably but are different stages — this
near-duplicate distinction is an intentional distractor.

## The distinction that matters

- **Dispute** — raised with the issuer; may be resolved by the merchant before any reversal.
- **Chargeback** — a scheme-driven forced refund with defined reason codes and evidence
  windows.

## Confusable neighbours

Chargeback and dispute are frequently confused with **friendly fraud**, where the
cardholder falsely claims a legitimate purchase was unauthorised. See `friendly-fraud.md`.
None of these are AML typologies; they belong to card operations and are present only to
test whether a retriever can keep card-fraud content out of a money-laundering answer.
